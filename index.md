### Human–AI Co-Creativity Consortium (HAI3C)

Human–AI Co-Creativity Consortium brings together researchers in AI, cognitive science, creativity research, mathematics and theoretical physics to pilot better ways for humans to interact with models, and to measure which ones keep people engaged and exploration diverse. Our goal is for humans and AI to work together more productively, without losing human skill and without losing the diversity of exploration. We build the infrastructure, datasets, and evaluation frameworks needed to train AI systems that think *with* us — not just *for* us.

### 🧠 Why should you care about this?

**Today's models are competent, but they are built as tools.** We hand over the assignment, go away, and come back for the result. By then the model has brainstormed with itself, and we are left reviewing the outcome instead of contributing to it. People think together differently: an idea, pushback, a tangent, building on it, agreeing to go forward. We want models that brainstorm *with* us while the work is still happening.

**Human exploration is diverse, and easy to lose.** Models are trained to explore the reasoning paths that lead to reward. People explore out of curiosity, in many directions at once, and many results come by chance. A person in the loop can go off on a tangent in the middle of the work; a model brainstorming with itself is guided by the distribution it learned. If people step out of the process, that diversity goes with them, and there will be no way to tell later what was lost.

**Models need a second training objective.** Beyond delivering results, they should be trained for productive collaboration with the human. This is an alignment question, and it matters most for the frontier models everyone will default to using. Since you cannot develop methods without measuring their impact, we measure every way of working we pilot.

### What we're building

- **The brainstorm arena** *(closed beta)*: our testbed for new ways of working with models. Mathematicians work with frontier models on open problems while we vary how the models take part (in different roles, several at once addressing each other and the human, speaking up unprompted), and we score each session on whether the human stays engaged and whether exploration stays diverse.
- **AGC-Bench** *(released)*: a meta-benchmark for measuring creativity in LLMs — 67 benchmarks across six domains, 83 models scored, an open-weight judge, and a public leaderboard. On human-matched tasks, the best human still beats the best model on novelty and diversity. [AGC-Bench on Hugging Face →](https://huggingface.co/agcbench-2026)
- **The CrowdMath dataset**: 164 research discussions from ten years of the MIT PRIMES CrowdMath program, each traced from an open problem to a finished proof, with every post labeled by experts for its role - progress, proof, error, etc. [CrowdMath paper →](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06526)
- **Next**: a public co-creativity benchmark that ranks models on how well they collaborate, scored on the human's share of the outcome; and post-training of open-weight models on collaborative sessions, as a proof of concept for the labs.
